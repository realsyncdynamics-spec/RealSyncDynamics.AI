# Website Transformation — Price Experiment

## Decision

Keep **€349 one-time** as the canonical/list price for the Website Transformation. Do not permanently reduce the product value to €249 based on intuition alone.

Use **€249 only as a controlled launch/market-test price**, if an experiment can be implemented without creating a second pricing source of truth.

## Why €349 remains defensible

The product is not a generic AI page generator. The paid transformation combines:

- existing-site evidence context
- governance/compliance findings
- Gemini reasoning
- four landing-page variants
- deterministic SiteOS rendering
- before/after preview
- backend preservation boundary
- governance validation before publish
- deployment/publish workflow

The commercial unit is therefore a **website transformation**, not an AI text-generation call.

## Why €249 is worth testing

The public funnel is self-service and value-first. A prospect has not necessarily built trust before seeing the generated result. A lower introductory price may reduce checkout friction.

This is a hypothesis, not a conclusion.

## Experiment rules

Do not run a simplistic 50/50 test that only measures checkout conversion. Measure the full economic funnel:

1. public domain entry
2. scan/analysis completed
3. new landing preview viewed
4. transformation CTA clicked
5. checkout started
6. payment completed
7. Core Governance subscription started within 30 days
8. additional capability/capacity purchase within 90 days
9. refund/cancellation rate

Primary metric:

`transformation revenue + expected 90-day expansion revenue per qualified visitor`

Secondary metrics:

- transformation conversion rate
- checkout conversion rate
- Core subscription attach rate
- 30/90-day retention
- refund rate
- support burden

## Recommended launch structure

**List price:** €349 one-time.

**Launch experiment:** €249 one-time for a defined cohort or acquisition period.

The UI must clearly distinguish a temporary launch price from the canonical value. Never maintain separate hard-coded frontend prices.

## Bundle principle

Do not use a large discount to hide the recurring subscription. The clean product story is:

`€349 Website Transformation` → `€79/month Core Governance`

The transformation creates the new site; the subscription keeps the website continuously monitored and governed.

A bundle may provide a modest incentive, but the recurring value must remain visible as a separate subscription.

## Guardrails

- No price change solely because a competitor is cheaper.
- Do not call the transformation a scan product.
- Do not compete with commodity AI website builders on raw generation price.
- Do not discount the transformation so heavily that it becomes a loss-leading service unless 90-day SaaS expansion economics justify it.
- Never hard-code experimental prices in UI code; all prices remain in the canonical pricing SSoT.

## Go/no-go interpretation

Keep €349 if it produces materially higher economic value per qualified visitor after considering subscription attach and retention.

Prefer €249 if conversion improves enough to compensate for the €100 lower transformation revenue and produces equal or higher 90-day contribution margin per qualified visitor.

If both are weak, do not simply lower the price again. Fix the value communication, preview quality, trust/evidence presentation, and checkout friction first.
